Asteroids

Dots are numbered minor planets with known discovery dates, shown in discovery order. New discoveries glow green, then settle to gray or their group color.

Fixed ellipses from Minor Planet Center orbital elements give approximate positions. The count covers the selected groups at the current date, not a live census.

Minor-planet groups

Near Earth
Orbits that come within 1.3 AU of the Sun.
Hungarias
Small, tilted orbits near the inner edge of the main belt.
Main belt
Mostly between Mars and Jupiter, split into inner, middle and outer zones by orbit size. The zones overlap in the view.
Hildas
Orbits near 4 AU, in the region of Jupiter’s 3:2 resonance.
Jupiter Trojans
Objects near Jupiter’s orbit, associated with the swarms ahead of and behind the planet.
Distant
Larger orbits beyond Jupiter, including Centaurs and trans-Neptunian objects.

These are approximate groupings from orbital elements, not official classifications or verified resonances. Group colors changes appearance, not selection: the main belt stays gray on All; belt presets paint its zones from light inner to dark outer.

Potentially hazardous asteroids are not a separate group: this catalogue has no hazard flag. Dwarf planets overlap orbital groups.

Data: Minor Planet Center via orrery-data.

Comets

Choose comets in Options to show their positions and paths. Colours identify individual comets; markers are enlarged for visibility.

Being listed in the catalogue does not mean a trajectory is available. Coverage varies by comet and date; the chooser explains unavailable entries.

Positions and paths use JPL Horizons predictions, interpolated between samples. Accuracy varies by comet and date. Paths show a limited time window, so their ends may not meet.

Data: JPL Horizons via OrreryData.
